Wallet signal: it finds the runners, it cannot pay for itself

Paul's hunch: repeat early buyers (anonymous KOLs, a different crew for each 8 hours) sit in most quick runners, so a reputation learned from the tape should gate or size the migration rule. Reputations were rebuilt every day from settled outcomes only, from 17,225 migrations since 24 August; no wallet list of any kind.

Verdict

Fail. The tape-learned wallet score does pick out quick runners (0.66 AUC, well above shuffled wallets), but no gate or size rule built on it clears the bar out of sample: the picked gate returns −0.2 pts per entry on the current rule and −1.0 on the no-stop variant over 12 days, and the best size rule (+2.1 per SOL, no stop) lost on the audit days.

Do selective early wallets turn up in most quick runners?

Yes, and that is the problem. With reputations built only from earlier, settled migrations, 89 % of quick runners had at least one selective early buyer (traded set 84 %), in every 8-hour block (00-08 UK 87 %, 08-16 UK 90 %, 16-24 UK 91 %). But so did 79 % of the migrations that never reached $100k. Selective buyers are everywhere; their presence roughly doubles the odds of a quick runner (13 % → 24 % across all priced migrations, 8 % → 13 % on coins the rule actually trades) without separating the trades that pay.

A different set for each 8 hours

Partly true. The 561 selective wallets with at least five selective appearances put 72 % of them in their busiest 8-hour block, against 51 % if their hours were shuffled (null p95 52 %): wallets do keep shifts. The shift crews overlap little (Jaccard 00-08 vs 08-16 0.28, 08-16 vs 16-24 0.30, 00-08 vs 16-24 0.24), and 16–24 UK carries 47 % of selective appearances for 36 % of migrations. Hour-local reputations (the UK hour ±2 h) predict about as well as global ones (§3).

The bulk, 80,583 wallets and 64 % of appearances, are repeat buyers with no edge. Across the threshold grid the bot count runs 44–234 and the selective count 1,279–6,376; copy-trader counts swing 4,314–12,047 with the lag, count and share rules. The separation is real in shape but soft at the edges: the classes are thresholds on one continuum, not natural clusters.

Do bots cluster on bad coins?

Mildly, on the traded set: sorted by bot share of early buyers, EV under the current rule is +8.4 % / +3.7 % / −0.1 % (low / middle / high third) and quick runners 15 % / 13 % / 10 %. Skipping the high-bot third (44 % of trades) is worth +0.0 pts (band −1.6 … +1.4) on the current rule and −1.4 without the stop: not a skip signal.

Coordinated dumps

Paul's autopsy of RENTOPOLY (15 Sep, outside this window): 98 wallets that had bought on the curve 3–13 minutes before migration sold inside two slots at +105 s, price $36k → $2.4k. Calibrated from the event, not from old tags: at least 20 distinct sellers in two consecutive slots with the price halving, any time in the first 10 minutes. It hit 29 % of priced migrations and 37 % of the coins the rule trades (median 33 sellers, price $21k → $2.9k). The RENTOPOLY pattern is the minority: in the median event none of the sellers had bought on the curve in the 10 minutes before migration (median share 0 %), and only 10 % of events are a curve-cohort exit (3.0 % of migrations).

Read: the dumps are a clock, not a reaction: 423 of 1050 land 5.5–6.5 minutes after the pool opens (median 375 s). The rule has already left by then — its 5-minute time stop exits around 305 s — so most dumps happen after our sale. Coins that later dump are still bad trades: under the current rule they return −8.1 % against +9.7 % for the rest, because they bleed before the cliff.

No. The dump time is not readable from the tape well enough to trade around it. A per-second model on the traded coins (cohort share already sold, cohort sell rate and its fade over 10 s, cohort re-buys, 20-s net flow, drawdown from the high, time since T0; leave-one-day-out) ranks the next 10 seconds at AUC 0.69 on coins that dump, but turned into an exit it costs −1.5 pts per entry on the current rule (p95 threshold; p99 −0.3, p90 −2.6) and −2.9 without the stop; almost all of the cost comes from trades that never dump (on the dump trades themselves it moves −0.4 pts). Time since T0 alone ranks at 0.47 inside the 5-minute window because the clock spike sits after it. And the cliff is not where the money is: one second before the dump the position is already at −38.7 %, ten seconds before −24.3 %. The coins that dump do peak first (median +63 % above entry before the cliff), but nothing flags the peak in time: the money is lost in the bleed between the peak and the cliff, where the stop and the target already act. The one practical lesson is the clock itself: a time stop later than about 5.5 minutes walks into the dump spike.

Method, limits, arms tried

  • Universe. One read-only pass over the Linux tier1 tape 24 Aug → 14 Sep (150 M lines, nice 19, idle I/O): 17,214 migrations (first graduated trade of a mint seen on the curve, graduated memory from 24 Aug), curve buys in the 10 minutes before each, pool rows for 10 minutes after; a second pass for curve sells 3–14 Sep. Priced (labelled) migrations: opening print $10k–$120k and no curve print above $60k just before T0 — 8,516 of 17,225. 5,043 detections complete a normal ~$41k curve and then open at millions of dollars in the tape's MC (a mis-scaled pool price); they carry no outcome, and the N48 universe list's 207 later re-detections of already-migrated coins were dropped.
  • Reputation. Early buyer = a buy in [T0 − 600 s, T0 + 3 s). For each appearance, the wallet's record over migrations whose 5-minute outcome settled before 00:00 UTC of the decision day: shrunk runner rate (h + a·p₀)/(n + a), lift over the base rate p₀, optional half-life, UK hour ±2 h profile, participation over its active window, sub-100 ms sniper share. Selective = ≥ 3 appearances, lift > 1, Wilson lower bound (z 1.28) above p₀, not a bot. Copy-traders are collapsed into their leader's cluster when both bought the coin. Missing history is unknown (trade kept), never zero.
  • Protocol. Develop 3–11 Sep: for each baseline, the arm with the highest develop Δ among arms whose day-clustered band is above zero (else the highest Δ); frozen; audited once on 12–14. Gates skip trades at or below the develop-day 20 / 33 / 50 % quantile of the score; sizes ×0.5 / ×1 / ×2 by develop thirds with the fills re-simulated at each clip (capital-neutral per original entry: size +1.4 current rule, +1.6 no stop). Baselines: the shadow's realistic leg, PumpSwap v1.2 tiered fees, entry first row ≥ T0 + 4.7 s, the −25 % stop or none. Shuffled-wallet null: 20 complete re-runs of all 24 reputation arms and the selection with wallet identities permuted within each day.
  • Arms tried. 540 wallet gate arms, 192 size arms, 16 N49 rough-score arms, 36 dump-tell gates, 18 farm-exit arms, 6 hazard-exit arms, 5 label definitions, a 54-cell class-threshold grid and 8 copy-trader rules. All are in the results files.
  • Limits. 12 trading days, 3 of them audit; one market regime; the tape's MC is the price (phantom prints guarded only by the two-print check); curve history before 24 Aug is missing, so early reputations are thin; wallets are tape identities — one person can run many. No curated list, KOL registry or WALLET-FORGE export was read.
