Migration Exit Lab: how much to want, and when to stop wanting it

The first three seconds after a pump.fun pool opens tell you whether to be in. This page is about what to do once you are: fixed targets, trailing stops, selling half and riding, time stops, dynamic targets, and the holder and timing tells that raise the odds of a 3× or 4× instead of a 2×. Every number is a simulation over real trades, fees included.

3

Time rules and dynamic targets

Your "if X doesn't happen by Y, it's probably Z" family, as exits. The one that works is the blunt one: a hard time stop. "If it hasn't doubled in 5 minutes, get out" beats waiting, because a coin that hasn't doubled by then reaches 2× later only 19% of the time and 3× only 8%.

Aiming for 3× and 4×: what raises the odds

Single tells measured at or before the 3-second entry, on held-line coins (base odds: 3× 23%, 4× 10%). Bars are the 3× odds; the vertical rule is the base. The last two columns show EV under two reference exits so a "high odds, bad price" tell is visible for what it is.

Pairs of tells

"Price at 3 s ≥ 1.15×" keeps showing up: the coins already 15% above the open three seconds in are the ones that run, but you pay that 15%. Fast migrator plus that print gives 3× odds of 45% and still +17% EV under stop 20 / target +100.

5

Holder behaviour around the line

Wallet-level, from the trade tape. "Run-up money" = wallets that bought on the bonding curve in the 15 minutes before migration; "new money" = wallets first seen after the pool opened.

The counter-intuitive one is real in this sample: when run-up holders dump 30%+ of their stake inside 30 s and the line still holds, that is new money absorbing supply, and those coins reach 3× at 37% versus 18% when the old money sits tight. Buyer count and buyer concentration carry no signal on their own; buy size in the first second does.

6

Entry timing and entry price

Five seconds is marginally better than three (the price is a little higher, the odds a little cleaner). Waiting 30–60 s to "confirm" costs the whole edge: entry drifts to 1.2–1.3× and the runners have already run. Buying the coins that dumped in the first 3 s looks tempting in aggregate but is flat or negative in every bucket; the aggregate is a few freak recoveries.

Testing the negative: what predicts the crash, and is it usable?

Same held-line trades, same base rule (stop −25%, target +100%, out at 5 min). "Crash" here is the trade being stopped out before it ever prints +50%. Two questions: does any tell known before entry raise those odds enough to skip the trade, and does any warning in the first 10–30 s after entry justify leaving early instead of waiting for the stop?

EV when you leave at 10 s because net flow turned negative, versus +13.7% waiting for the stop. Every early-exit overlay tested is worse than the plain stop: too many winners dip first.

Reading the table: "lift" is the tell's stop-out rate over the base. The post-entry warnings (dip below entry, negative flow) do predict the crash at 70–75%, yet excluding those trades after the fact is not the same as exiting on them: by the time the warning is visible you are already near the stop, and the ones that recover pay for the ones that don't. Verdict for decision-making: the crash tells are close to irrelevant pre-entry, and as exits they are dominated by the fixed stop. Spend the attention on the upside tells and the clock.

9

Method, limits, next

  • Data. Every trade in [pool creation −15 min, +30 min] for 974 pump.fun migrations captured by the engine (2026-09-03 → 09-14), millisecond timestamps. Prices are the engine's market-cap print per trade. The line is the pool's first trade (median $33.9k); the bonding curve completes ~25% above it, which is the on-screen "31k vs 41k" gap.
  • Entry. First trade ≥ 3 s after the open, only if no trade printed below 0.95× the open inside those 3 s. Fills are assumed at that trade's price; the opening pool is thin, so keep clips small and expect slippage above what is modelled here.
  • Exits. Simulated on the trade path with the same step order for every rule: time stop, scale-out sells, target, time check, stop (only while nothing has been scaled out), trailing stop (only once the activation gain has printed). Fees 2.5% per round trip; partial sells are charged pro rata.
  • Not modelled. Execution latency, slippage, failed transactions, sandwiches. All of these hurt the fast rules most.
  • Sample. 12 days. Cuts with n under 40 are shown dimmed or omitted. Treat pairs of tells as leads, not rules.
  • Next. Shadow-log the 3-second hold live off the engine's own trade feed with the 5-minute time stop, then a look at which sniper wallets open the good ones.
