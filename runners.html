Runner squeeze: the peaks are real, the leak is the stop

Paul's question, from a +79 % win on a coin that later peaked at +511 %: can we squeeze more out of the runners, for instance by selling the initials around +80–100 % and riding the rest, and can we tell in advance which ones will run: new holders, volume, wallets, shelves, chart structure, the coin's tempo, and the time it took to migrate? Every number below is a simulation of the shadow book's real fills over 1,969 trades, 3–14 September, on PumpSwap's tiered fees.

Verdict

Selling part and riding the rest

Astra's order: one fixed rule first, called R30. Sell half at the first +100 % trigger; the rest exits when its sale value falls 30 % from its best since the trigger, or below its share of our entry cost, and no later than 10 minutes after the open. Then stress it, measure the ceiling, and only then vary the fraction, the deadline, the mechanism and the first target.

Read: dots are the extra profit per trade against selling everything at +100 %, over all 1,969 trades (trades that never touch +100 % count as zero); the bar is the 95 % band from resampling whole days. Everything left of zero loses money. The dashed line is the +2-point bar a rule had to clear. R30 lost on 11 of 12 days.

Why it fails: after the first half is sold, the remainder of the typical winner exits at 0.83× the +100 % price, so 75 % of winners give back part of the gain. Only 28 % of touches end better than selling everything. The few that run far do not repay the many that fall straight back. Slower execution does not rescue it (-1.7 pts at a 3-second delay) and an extra 2 % haircut on every sale changes nothing (-2.0 pts). Selling more up front loses less (33 %: -2.8 pts, 50 %: -2.0 pts, 66 %: -1.4 pts, 100 %: 0), which is the same result read the other way. Holding to 30 minutes instead of 10 costs a further 0.2 pts. Paul's version, selling exactly the stake plus costs (about 53 % of the bag at +100 %) and riding the house money, lands on top of R30 at -2.0 pts.

The one change in this family with a lower bound above zero is not a partial sale at all: sell everything at +150 % instead of +100 % (+1.1 pts, band +0.0 … +2.6). It is under the +2-point bar, and after 302 arms one lower bound grazing zero is what luck alone produces, so it stays a lead for the forward book rather than a rule.

  • Volume. Net buy SOL in the last 30 seconds: at +50 % the top third reached +100 % 52 % vs 38 % (1.35×, 11/12 days). At the touch, 62 % vs 50 % reach 3×, but the audit days reversed it. Weak, and no decision changes.

The fee correction

The first version of this lab, and the shadow book up to v1.1, charged a flat 0.30 % pool fee per swap. PumpSwap's fees are tiered by market cap in SOL: 1.25 % under 420 SOL, 1.20 % to 1,470 SOL, 1.15 % to 2,460 SOL, falling to 0.30 % only above 98,240 SOL, and charged on the SOL side of every swap. On the old model the baseline reproduces run 4 exactly (+4.96 % over 1,969 trades vs the shadow's +4.97 %; 1,955 trades identical to 6 decimals). On the tiered schedule it is +3.02 % (band +0.5 … +5.5); the shadow's own v1.2 fee migration of the stored run-4 fills gives +3.03 %. Entries pay 1.25 % (1,840 of 1,969); because pools open just under the 420-SOL line, 796 exits pay 1.20 %. Method, limits, arms tried

  • Sample. 1,969 shadow-book entries on 3–14 September (run 4's traded set), every trade in [T0 − 60 s, T0 + 30 min], bonding-curve history back to 24 August. Entry fills at the first trade at least 4.7 s after the open (3 s gate, 0.2 s detection, 1.5 s execution); every exit fills at the first trade at least 1.5 s after its trigger, or at the last known pool state if none arrives within 30 s; constant-product impact from the pool reserves; 0.000205 SOL per transaction; 0.25 SOL clip; tiered PumpSwap fees through the shadow's own v1.2 fill functions.
  • Scoring. Every rule is compared with selling everything at +100 % on the same trades, averaged over all trades (untouched trades count as zero), with 2,000 whole-day bootstrap resamples, the best day and best three coins removed, and a +2-point-per-trade bar with a lower band above zero.
  • Models. Features are frozen at the decision instant (T0 + 3.2 s, the first +50 % print, the +100 % trigger print); the 1.5 s after a trigger belongs to execution. Fits expand chronologically (3–5 → 6, then one more day at a time through 11), are frozen, and are audited once on 12–14; leave-one-day-out is a secondary check; nulls shuffle whole payoff vectors within day and fast/slow-touch groups (1,000) or labels within day (200).
  • Arms tried. 302 exit and stop arms in the verdict chart (277 ride-the-rest variants including 2 execution stresses, 2 first-target changes, 17 stop rules, 6 viewer-conditioned stops), 765 second-chance arms in section 5, and 66 model or tercile selections (Astra's 12 threshold × fraction cells, 2 gated stop rules, the family and viewer tercile tests, 6 snapshot rules). Every attempted exit, stop and second-chance arm is in a table view.
  • Historical discovery. The ideas were designed with 12–14 September already on the tape, so the audit days are a check, not a confirmation. Confirmation needs days the design never saw: the shadow book should carry a no-stop leg forward before any rule change.
  • Limits. Twelve days; tail risk is weakly measured. The tape keeps trades above $1,000 market cap only, so a coin that collapses below that fills against its last recorded pool state; this flatters riding and holding slightly, which strengthens the negative results and slightly weakens the no-stop result. Buyers are wallets that bought on the tape, not holders; transfers and wallet splitting are invisible. Viewer data comes from our ranked-feed journal and exists only while a coin is in the trending table (about a fifth of trades at migration).
  • Corrections to the first pass. (1) The fee model now uses the shadow's own v1.2 functions (official 25-tier ladder, tier on the pool's market cap from reserves, fee added on top of the buy); the earlier tiered version assumed the ladder above tier 4 and priced the tier from USD. Baseline moved from +3.01 % to +3.02 %. (2) The shadow's fill module was rewritten to v1.2 at 19:34 UTC, after which the lab's '0.30 %' mode would silently have charged the tiered schedule; the lab now carries its own copy of the old model. (3) Wallet track records counted outcomes of earlier migrations whose 10-minute window had not closed at the decision; they now use only finished outcomes.
